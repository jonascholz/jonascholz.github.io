A world action model (WAM) adapts a video generation model for robotics. This is a competing approach to a vision language action model (VLA), which adapts vision language models.

## Motivation

Video generation requires a pretty sophisticated understanding of the world. Consider the banana in [Figure 1](#fig:falling). If we ask a model to generate the next frame in this sequence, it has to make sense of what's happening here. The banana is in mid-air, therefore it is likely pulled down by gravity. The distance the banana covers until the next frame would be different for another object such as a feather.

<figure id="fig:falling">
<Diagram name="falling-objects-diagram" />
<figcaption><strong>Figure 1:</strong> To predict the next frame, the model has to know that a banana falls faster and faster, while a feather drifts down slowly.</figcaption>
</figure>

In general, generating future frames requires understanding the mechanics of the world to some extent. Just as this knowledge of the world can be used to predict banana dynamics, we can use it to predict robot dynamics. In fact, we can use this world understanding already encoded in the model weights to predict the robot actions that reach a target state.

From the vision language side, there is also an understanding of the world. But it is less visual. Most of the training data is text and some of it is text-image pairs. The output is almost always text, as in [Figure 2](#fig:vlm).

## Input and output formats

Let's assume everything is evenly matched: both models have the same number of parameters $\cN{N}$ and see the same context, so there are as many input tokens for the WAM as there are for the VLA. We call this number $\cnc{n_c}$; it covers the text prompt and the current camera images. The context will look like [Figure 3](#fig:context).

<figure id="fig:context">
<Diagram name="wam-vla-context-diagram" props={{ context: 800, future: 588, actions: 50 }} />
<figcaption><strong>Figure 3:</strong> Token sequences of a VLA and a WAM with the same context, drawn to scale for the example numbers used in this post. Both denoise an action chunk; the WAM also denoises future frames.</figcaption>
</figure>

<details>
<summary>Note, in practice these are not evenly matched!</summary>

WAMs encode images with the video model's VAE, VLAs with a ViT. At 224×224 the counts are similar: 256 tokens with SigLIP [@paligemma], 196 with Wan2.1 [@wan]. But video models run at higher resolutions (one 480p frame is 1560 tokens), while their VAE packs 4 frames into one latent frame, which makes a history of frames cheap.

</details>

For the output, we usually predict an action chunk that contains the next $\cna{n_a}$ actions, for example $\cna{n_a} = 50$ in π0.5 [@pi05]. With flow matching or diffusion, the chunk enters the sequence as noisy tokens and the model learns to denoise them. However, this does not account for auxiliary losses.

## Training costs

A WAM will often use next-frame prediction as an auxiliary loss. This requires many more output tokens than a VLA. And they clog up the input token context, too, since we condition on these noisy frames: just like the noisy actions, the noisy future frames are part of the sequence the model processes.

To put a number on it, we use the usual rule of thumb that a forward and backward pass costs about 6 FLOPs per parameter per token [@kaplan]. We ignore attention for now. With $\cnf{n_f}$ future frame tokens, the training cost per sample is

$$
\begin{align}
C_\text{train}^\text{VLA} &\approx 6\cN{N}\left(\cnc{n_c} + \cna{n_a}\right) \label{eq:train_vla} \\
C_\text{train}^\text{WAM} &\approx 6\cN{N}\left(\cnc{n_c} + \cnf{n_f} + \cna{n_a}\right) \label{eq:train_wam}
\end{align}
$$

Let's plug in some numbers. A π0-style VLA with three cameras at 224×224 has $3 \times 256 = 768$ image tokens, so with a short prompt $\cnc{n_c} \approx 800$. If the WAM predicts one future latent frame per camera with a Wan-style tokenizer, that is $\cnf{n_f} = 3 \times 196 = 588$ tokens. With $\cna{n_a} = 50$, the ratio of $\eqref{eq:train_wam}$ to $\eqref{eq:train_vla}$ is

$$
\frac{800 + 588 + 50}{800 + 50} \approx 1.7
$$

So in this setup the auxiliary loss makes every training sample about 70% more expensive, and that is the cheap case: one latent frame at a low resolution. More future frames or the native 480p resolution of the video model grow $\cnf{n_f}$ quickly. And the attention cost we ignored grows with the square of the sequence length, so it penalizes the longer WAM sequence even more.

## Inference costs

We assume the WAM does not have to generate the future frames at inference, for example by decoding actions and video with separate heads as in UVA [@uva]. Then both models process the same tokens: the context once, and the action chunk at each of the $\cK{K}$ denoising steps. A forward pass costs about 2 FLOPs per parameter per token [@kaplan], so for both

$$
\begin{equation}
C_\text{inf} \approx 2\cN{N}\left(\cnc{n_c} + \cK{K}\cna{n_a}\right)
\label{eq:inf}
\end{equation}
$$

The extra cost of a WAM is all in training.

<details>
<summary>What if the WAM generates the frames anyway?</summary>

Then every denoising step also processes the $\cnf{n_f}$ frame tokens, $2\cN{N}(\cnc{n_c} + \cK{K}(\cnf{n_f} + \cna{n_a}))$, which is about $5.5\times$ $\eqref{eq:inf}$ with $\cK{K} = 10$ and the numbers above. Cosmos Policy (2B) does this and takes about 0.61 s per prediction on an H100 with 5 denoising steps [@cosmospolicy], while π0 takes 73 ms on an RTX 4090 [@pi0]. DreamZero (14B) needed a 38× system speedup to reach about 7 Hz [@dreamzero].

</details>


## Takeaways

- **Training:** the future frame loss makes the sequence longer, about $1.7\times$ the compute per sample in our example, and more with more frames, higher resolutions or the attention cost.
- **Inference:** if the WAM skips the future frames, it costs the same as a VLA.
- **Evenly matched is rare:** in practice WAMs are built on large video backbones (2B to 14B parameters in the examples above) at video resolutions, while VLAs like π0 pair a 3B VLM with a small action expert. Both push the real gap further in the VLA's favor.
